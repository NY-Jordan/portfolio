Building a voice AI receptionist is a different kind of hard than building a web app. The failure modes are audible, and users notice a half-second of latency the way they'd never notice it on a page load.

## Latency is a feature, not an optimization

In a chat interface, a two-second delay reads as "thinking." On a phone call, a two-second delay reads as a dropped line. Every component in the pipeline — speech-to-text, the language model, text-to-speech — has to be budgeted in milliseconds, not just correctness.

## The conversation doesn't end when the model responds

A web form either has the right answer or it doesn't. A phone conversation has interruptions, half-finished sentences, and callers who change their mind mid-request. Handling that gracefully matters more than handling the happy path perfectly.

## What's next

This is early — the real lessons will come from callers, not from design docs. Future posts here will get more specific as Callora handles more real conversations.
